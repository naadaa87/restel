When people hear "space rental" in Seoul, they usually think of party rooms, photo studios or meeting rooms — places you book in order to do something. A rest space rental is the opposite. You book it to do nothing at all.

## Why you might need one

Cities are full of places to sit and surprisingly short of places to lie down. A café gives you a chair, not a quiet corner to close your eyes. Your hotel may be across town. The airport is hours away.

Think about the gaps in a day: a long lunch break, two hours between appointments, the last afternoon of a trip when your flight leaves at night. A rest space rental is a room for exactly those gaps — somewhere private where you can dim the lights, take your shoes off and lie flat for a while.

## How it differs from a hotel

It is the question we hear most, so here is the short answer: RESTEL is not a hotel, and it is not lodging of any kind. Resting and staying turn out to be quite different things.

| | Hotel | Rest space rental (RESTEL) |
|---|---|---|
| Booked by | The night | 60 minutes, 3 hours, up to 8 hours |
| Hours | Around the clock | 07:00–24:00; every session ends before midnight |
| Where you lie down | Bed and duvet | Folding floor mats and a blanket instead |
| Washing | Bathroom and shower | A washbasin, no shower |
| What it is for | Sleeping and staying | Napping, meditating, resetting |

There are no beds at RESTEL. You lie on folding mats on a raised wooden platform, with a blanket and a buckwheat pillow. We deliberately left out what you would need to spend a night and kept only what you need to rest: three light levels, nature sounds, guided meditation and a cup of tea.

## The moments it is made for

- After an early landing, when your room across town isn't ready and you can't face sightseeing yet
- Between a morning of walking and an evening out in Hongdae
- Before a late flight, once you've left your hotel and have hours to fill
- In the middle of a long workday, for a proper nap rather than a slump at your desk
- Any time you simply want an hour with your phone face down

What these have in common is that they are short. You don't need somewhere to stay. You need somewhere to catch your breath.

## Why "tel"?

Hotel, motel, hostel — in Korea, as in many places, "-tel" sounds like a place to stay. We kept the ending and put REST in front of it. RESTEL is meant to be the fourth "tel".

You still get what people like about a room of their own: a door that closes and a set amount of time that belongs to you. What changes is how you use it. Nobody unpacks at RESTEL. You take your shoes off, lower the lights and lie down.

## How resting works at RESTEL

RESTEL Sinchon offers three lengths of session: Nap 60 for a quick sleep, Rest 180 for an unhurried afternoon, and Day Rest for up to eight hours within opening times. For travellers there is also K-REST 90, a Korean rest experience built around tea, guided breathing and rest on a warm floor.

Whichever you choose, the whole space is yours — alone or with the people you came with. We leave time between sessions so you won't bump into anyone else. RESTEL is for ages 19 and over, and there is no smoking, alcohol or cooking.

Resting needs a place too. That simple idea is where RESTEL began.
